What is ML · supervised vs unsupervised

S1–S2
WhatMachine ko rules haath se nahi likhte; data se pattern seekhne dete hain. Program experience (data) se task pe better hota jaata hai.
WhyKuch problems ke rules likhna impossible hai (kaunsa student risk pe hai?). Data mein pattern hai, model use pakad leta hai. Exam mein 5-mark "define + types + examples" pakka aata hai.
When"Define ML", "differentiate supervised and unsupervised", "classify these tasks" type wording.
HowLabel hai? → supervised (number = regression, category = classification). Label nahi? → unsupervised (clustering, dimensionality reduction, density estimation).
A computer program is said to learn from experience E with respect to some class of tasks T and performance measure P, if its performance at tasks in T, as measured by P, improves with experience E (Mitchell). In supervised learning the training data contain input–target pairs (xn, tn) and the goal is to predict t for a new x; in unsupervised learning only inputs xn are given and the goal is to discover structure such as clusters, low-dimensional representations or the density p(x).
AspectSupervisedUnsupervised
Data(x, t) pairs, labelledonly x, unlabelled
Goalpredict t for unseen xfind structure / compress / model p(x)
Tasksregression (t real), classification (t discrete)clustering, dimensionality reduction, density estimation
Algorithms (this course)linear regression, logistic, LDA, SVM, CARTK-Means, GMM + EM, hierarchical, PCA, factor analysis
Evaluationaccuracy, F1, ROC-AUC, RMSE, R² on held-out datasilhouette, Davies–Bouldin, BIC, likelihood, ARI (if labels exist)
Both types on the same data. Supervised: predict at_risk (classification, CV AUC 0.953) and end-semester SGPA (regression, R² 0.798). Unsupervised: K-Means finds 3 personas (High risk / Coasting / Engaged achievers) without ever seeing the label. The HMM in Unit 4 is unsupervised too: hidden states are never labelled.
"Generalisation" means performance on unseen data, not training data. A model with 100 % training accuracy can still be useless (overfitting, topic 02).

02

Polynomial curve fitting · overfitting · regularisation

S3 · B1 §1.1
WhatPoints ke beech ek polynomial curve fit karna: y(x, w) = w0 + w1x + … + wMxM. M = order (flexibility).
WhyYe ML ka "hello world" hai: underfitting vs overfitting aur regularisation yahin samajh aata hai. Har unit isi idea pe wapas aata hai.
When"Explain overfitting with polynomial curve fitting", "role of M / λ / data size", "sum-of-squares error".
HowError E(w) = ½ Σ (y(xn, w) − tn)² minimise karo (linear in w → closed form). Test error dekho; M badhao to train error ↓ but test error pehle ↓ phir ↑.
Polynomial curve fitting models y(x, w) = Σj=0..M wjxj, which is nonlinear in x but linear in w, and chooses w by minimising the sum-of-squares error E(w) = ½ Σn {y(xn, w) − tn}². A model that fits the training data closely but generalises poorly is overfitting; it is controlled by more data, a lower order M, or regularisation Ẽ(w) = E(w) + (λ/2)‖w‖², which penalises large coefficients.

RMSE = √(2E/N) use karo: N se divide karke alag size ke datasets compare ho jaate hain, aur units t jaise hi rehte hain.
Overfitting ka matlab "M bada hai" nahi hai. M = 9 with 1000 points is fine; M = 9 with 10 points interpolates noise. Data size vs flexibility ka ratio matter karta hai.

W2 · Why does a 9th-order polynomial through 10 points have zero training error but huge test error?

Show solution
• A polynomial of order M has M+1 = 10 free coefficients. • With N = 10 points, the 10 linear equations y(x_n, w) = t_n have an exact solution → E(w*) = 0. [interpolation] • The coefficients become very large with alternating signs to pass through every noisy point, so between points the curve oscillates wildly → large test error. [overfitting] • Remedies: more data (N ≫ M), lower M chosen by validation, or regularisation λ‖w‖²/2.
03

Probability rules · Bayes' theorem · independence

S4–S5 · B2 §2.2
WhatDo hi rules: sum rule p(X) = ΣY p(X, Y) aur product rule p(X, Y) = p(Y|X) p(X). Bayes inhi dono se nikalta hai.
WhyHar probabilistic model (NB, LDA, GMM, HMM) inhi do rules pe chalta hai. 5-mark "state and derive Bayes" + 10-mark numerical common hai.
When"Given prior and likelihood find posterior", "test is 90 % accurate…", "are A and B independent?", "conditional independence".
HowPosterior = likelihood × prior / evidence. Evidence = Σ over all H (sum rule). Independence check: P(A∩B) =? P(A)P(B).
Bayes' theorem: P(H | E) = P(E | H) P(H) / P(E), where P(E) = Σh P(E | h) P(h). Here P(H) is the prior, P(E | H) the likelihood, P(E) the evidence and P(H | E) the posterior. Events A and B are independent if P(A, B) = P(A) P(B); they are conditionally independent given C if P(A, B | C) = P(A | C) P(B | C).

P(E | H) ≠ P(H | E). "90 % of at-risk students have low attendance" does not mean "90 % of low-attendance students are at risk". The prior (base rate) changes everything.
Naive Bayes = assume features are conditionally independent given the class: p(x1, …, xD | C) = Π p(xi | C). Galat assumption bhi often achha classifier deta hai.
H = at risk, E1 = attendance below SRM's 75 % rule, E2 = internals below 60 %. Prior 0.28 → after E1 0.71 → after E2 0.96. Exact P(H | E1, E2) from data is 0.963, so assuming E1 ⟂ E2 | H costs only 0.002 here. Low attendance and low study hours are dependent overall (P(A∩B) = 0.175 vs P(A)P(B) = 0.117) but much closer to independent once risk is known: risk is the common cause.

Worked examples

W1 · 28 % of students are at risk. 61.3 % of at-risk students have attendance below 75 %, and 24 % of all students do. Find P(at risk | attendance below 75 %).

Show solution
Let H = at risk, E = attendance < 75 %. P(H) = 0.28 (prior), P(E | H) = 0.613 (likelihood), P(E) = 0.24 (evidence) P(H | E) = P(E | H) P(H) / P(E) [Bayes' theorem] = 0.613 × 0.2775 / 0.24 = 0.708 ∴ About 71 % of students with attendance below 75 % are at risk (vs 28 % overall).

W2 · An early-warning test flags 90 % of at-risk students and 10 % of safe students. Base rate 28 %. A student is flagged: probability they are at risk?

Show solution
P(F | R) = 0.9, P(F | ¬R) = 0.1, P(R) = 0.28, P(¬R) = 0.72 P(F) = P(F | R)P(R) + P(F | ¬R)P(¬R) [sum rule + product rule] = 0.9 × 0.28 + 0.1 × 0.72 = 0.252 + 0.072 = 0.324 P(R | F) = 0.252 / 0.324 = 0.778 [Bayes' theorem] ∴ Even a "90 % accurate" flag is right only 77.8 % of the time; 22.2 % of flags are false alarms.

W3 · P(A) = 0.24, P(B) = 0.4875, P(A ∩ B) = 0.175. Are A and B independent?

Show solution
P(A)P(B) = 0.24 × 0.4875 = 0.117 P(A ∩ B) = 0.175 ≠ 0.117 [definition of independence] ∴ A and B are NOT independent (they occur together more often than chance: positively dependent).
04

Continuous RVs · quantiles · mean, variance · expectation · covariance

S6–S7 · B2 §2.2, B1 §1.2
WhatContinuous variable (CGPA, attendance) ke liye probability density p(x); P(a<X<b) = ∫p(x)dx. Quantile = CDF ka inverse.
WhyGaussian har jagah hai: regression noise, LDA, GMM, Kalman. Mean, variance, covariance = EDA ki language (PBL-I ke 4 marks).
When"Find expectation and variance", "compute covariance / correlation", "median / quartiles", "probability from a normal distribution".
HowE[X] = Σ x p(x) (discrete) or ∫ x p(x) dx. Var = E[X²] − E[X]². Cov = E[(X − μx)(Y − μy)], r = Cov / (σxσy).
For a continuous random variable X with probability density p(x) ≥ 0 and ∫p(x)dx = 1, the cumulative distribution is F(x) = P(X ≤ x) and the q-quantile is F−1(q) (median = F−1(0.5)). The expectation of f is E[f] = ∫ p(x) f(x) dx, the variance is var[x] = E[x²] − E[x]², and the covariance of x and y is cov[x, y] = E[(x − E[x])(y − E[y])]; the correlation r = cov / (σxσy) lies in [−1, 1].
CGPA in the cohort, modelled as N(7.32, 1.34²). Shaded: P(CGPA < 6) = 0.161 under the normal model; the data say 0.215 (left tail heavier than Gaussian).
Correlation sirf linear relation pakadta hai. r ≈ 0 ka matlab "no relation" nahi, sirf "no linear relation". Aur r scale-free hai, covariance nahi.
Sample variance/covariance mein N − 1 se divide karo (unbiased). Sirf population formula pucha ho to N.
EDA (PBL-I rubric 4 marks): CGPA mean 7.32, variance 1.79, quartiles 6.24 / 7.31 / 8.45. Strongest correlation with at_risk: internal marks r = −0.67, attendance −0.59, backlogs +0.41. Covariance pairs: attendance ↔ internals cov 73.98, r 0.625.

05

MLE = least squares · ridge · robust regression

S10–S11 · B2 §7.3–7.5
WhatLinear regression t = wᵀφ(x) + noise. Noise Gaussian maano to likelihood maximise karna = squared error minimise karna.
Why"Least squares kyun?" ka asli answer yahi hai. Ridge = overfitting control; robust = outliers se bachao.
When"Derive least squares from MLE", "normal equations", "ridge regression", "effect of outliers", "Huber / Laplace loss".
HowwML = (ΦᵀΦ)−1Φᵀt. Ridge: (λI + ΦᵀΦ)−1Φᵀt. Robust: replace squared loss with |r| or Huber.
With t = wᵀφ(x) + ε, ε ~ N(0, β−1), the log-likelihood is ln p(t | w, β) = (N/2) ln β − (N/2) ln 2π − β ED(w) with ED = ½Σ(tn − wᵀφn)², so maximum likelihood ≡ least squares: wML = (ΦᵀΦ)−1Φᵀt (the normal equations) and 1/βML = (1/N)Σ(tn − wMLᵀφn)². Ridge regression minimises ED + (λ/2)‖w‖², giving w = (λI + ΦᵀΦ)−1Φᵀt. Robust regression uses a heavy-tailed noise model (Laplace → absolute loss, or Huber loss) so that outliers have bounded influence.
Squared loss grows like r², so one outlier dominates. Huber is quadratic near 0 and linear in the tails, so outliers are bounded.
Intercept w0 ko ridge penalty mat do (data centre karo, ya penalty matrix mein w0 ka entry 0 rakho). Warna sab predictions zero ki taraf shrink honge.
Ridge mein features standardise karo pehle. Warna penalty unit pe depend karegi (CGPA vs attendance %).
Target: end-semester SGPA. OLS (normal equations, matches sklearn exactly) R² 0.790; Ridge (α chosen by CV) R² 0.798 best. Robustness test: corrupt 8 % of training targets → OLS test RMSE 0.668 → 0.839, Huber 0.678 → 0.675 (barely moves).

Worked examples

W1 · Same 4 points (1,2), (2,3), (3,5), (4,4). Fit ridge with λ = 1 (intercept unpenalised) and compare the slope with OLS.

Show solution
Centre: x̄ = 2.5, ȳ = 3.5 x − x̄ = (−1.5, −0.5, 0.5, 1.5), y − ȳ = (−1.5, −0.5, 1.5, 0.5) Sxx = Σ(x − x̄)² = 5, Sxy = Σ(x − x̄)(y − ȳ) = 2.25 + 0.25 + 0.75 + 0.75 = 4 OLS slope w1 = Sxy / Sxx = 4/5 = 0.8 Ridge slope w1 = Sxy / (Sxx + λ) = 4/6 = 0.667 [shrunk towards 0] Ridge intercept w0 = ȳ − w1 x̄ = 3.5 − 0.667 × 2.5 = 1.833 ∴ ridge: y = 1.833 + 0.667x (OLS: y = 1.5 + 0.8x). Larger λ → flatter line.

W2 · Show that maximising the Gaussian likelihood is the same as minimising the sum-of-squares error.

Show solution
p(t | X, w, β) = Π_n N(t_n | wᵀφ_n, β⁻¹) [i.i.d. 06

Bayesian linear regression

S11 · B1 §3.3, B2 §7.6
Whatw ko ek number nahi, ek distribution maano. Prior N(0, α−1I) × likelihood → posterior N(mN, SN).
WhyPrediction ke saath uncertainty milti hai ("SGPA 6.1 ± 1.4"). Overfitting automatically kam (prior = regulariser).
When"Derive posterior over w", "predictive distribution", "relation between MAP and ridge", "evidence approximation".
HowSN−1 = αI + βΦᵀΦ, mN = βSNΦᵀt. Predict: mean mNᵀφ(x), variance 1/β + φᵀSNφ.
In Bayesian linear regression a Gaussian prior p(w) = N(w | 0, α−1I) is combined with the Gaussian likelihood to give the posterior p(w | t) = N(w | mN, SN) with SN−1 = αI + βΦᵀΦ and mN = βSNΦᵀt. The predictive distribution is p(t | x, t) = N(t | mNᵀφ(x), σN²(x)) with σN²(x) = 1/β + φ(x)ᵀSNφ(x): noise plus parameter uncertainty. The posterior mode equals the ridge solution with λ = α/β.
Predictive variance ke do parts: 1/β = data ka noise (kabhi kam nahi hoga), φᵀSNφ = model ki uncertainty (data badhne se → 0).
MAP estimate Bayesian nahi hai, woh sirf ridge hai. "Fully Bayesian" = poore posterior pe average karke predict karna.
Implemented from scratch with evidence maximisation: α = 0.289, β = 2.27 (noise σ = 0.66 SGPA), effective parameters γ = 15.98 of 16. 93 % of test students fall inside the 95 % predictive band: the uncertainty is honest.

Worked examples

W1 · One weight, no bias: x = (1, 2, 3), t = (2, 3, 5), α = 1, β = 1. Find the posterior over w and the prediction at x = 4.

Show solution
φ(x) = x, so ΦᵀΦ = Σx² = 1 + 4 + 9 = 14, Φᵀt = Σxt = 2 + 6 + 15 = 23 S_N⁻¹ = α + β Σx² = 1 + 14 = 15 → S_N = 1/15 = 0.0667 [posterior precision] m_N = β S_N Σxt = 23/15 = 1.533 [posterior mean] (compare w_ML = 23/14 = 1.643: the prior pulls w towards 0) Prediction at x = 4: mean = m_N × 4 = 6.133 variance = 1/β + x² S_N = 1 + 16/15 = 2.067 → std = 1.44 ∴ t(4) ≈ 6.13 ± 1.96 × 1.44 (95 % interval).
07

Linear classification: discriminant functions · generative vs discriminative

S12–S13 · B1 §4.1–4.3
WhatDiscriminant: y(x) = wᵀx + w0; y > 0 → class 1. Generative: p(x | C) aur p(C) model karo, Bayes se p(C | x). Discriminative: seedha p(C | x) = σ(wᵀφ).
WhyInterview/viva ka favourite: "LDA vs logistic regression". 5-mark compare question pakka.
When"Explain discriminant functions", "generative vs discriminative", "derive LDA boundary", "logistic sigmoid".
HowShared covariance Gaussian class-conditionals → posterior = σ(linear) → linear boundary (LDA). Logistic regression fits the same form directly by maximum likelihood.
A discriminant function y(x) = wᵀx + w0 assigns x to C1 if y(x) ≥ 0; the decision boundary y(x) = 0 is a hyperplane orthogonal to w at distance −w0/‖w‖ from the origin. A probabilistic generative model specifies p(x | Ck) and p(Ck) and obtains p(Ck | x) by Bayes' theorem; with Gaussian class-conditionals sharing a covariance this gives p(C1 | x) = σ(wᵀx + w0) (LDA). A probabilistic discriminative model such as logistic regression models p(C1 | x) = σ(wᵀφ(x)) directly and fits w by maximum likelihood.

Parameters (D features)means + shared covariance: O(D²)w: D + 1

Logistic regression classification model hai, naam mein "regression" hai sirf isliye ki ye probability (continuous) predict karta hai.
5-fold CV ROC-AUC: logistic regression 0.953, LDA (generative) 0.950, Gaussian Naive Bayes 0.925. NB is weakest because survey features are correlated (CGPA, previous SGPA, internals), which breaks its conditional-independence assumption.

Worked examples

W1 · CGPA | not-at-risk ~ N(7.8, 0.8²), CGPA | at-risk ~ N(5.9, 0.8²). Find the decision threshold (a) with equal priors, (b) with priors 0.72 / 0.28.

Show solution
Boundary: π₁ N(x | μ₁, σ²) = π₀ N(x | μ₀, σ²) [equal posteriors] Take logs, σ² cancels in the quadratic term (shared variance → linear boundary): x* = (μ₀ + μ₁)/2 + σ² ln(π₁/π₀) / (μ₀ − μ₁) (a) π₀ = π₁: x* = (7.8 + 5.9)/2 = 6.85 (b) x* = 6.85 + 0.64 × ln(0.28/0.72) / 1.9 = 6.85 + 0.64 × (−0.944)/1.9 = 6.85 − 0.318 = 6.53 ∴ Rarer at-risk class → threshold moves towards the at-risk mean (flag CGPA < 6.53).

W2 · Why does a shared covariance give a linear boundary but separate covariances give a quadratic one?

Show solution
ln p(x | C_k) = −½ (x − μ_k)ᵀ Σ_k⁻¹ (x − μ_k) + const_k Expand: −½ xᵀΣ_k⁻¹x + μ_kᵀΣ_k⁻¹x − ½ μ_kᵀΣ_k⁻¹μ_k Log-odds = ln p(x|C₁) − ln p(x|C₀) + ln(π₁/π₀) If Σ₁ = Σ₀ = Σ, the quadratic terms xᵀΣ⁻¹x cancel → log-odds linear in x [LDA] If Σ₁ ≠ Σ₀, xᵀ(Σ₀⁻¹ − Σ₁⁻¹)x remains → quadratic boundary [QDA]
08

Laplace approximation · Bayesian logistic regression

S14 · B1 §4.4–4.5
WhatLogistic regression ka posterior Gaussian nahi hota. Laplace: mode pe ek Gaussian fit kar do, curvature (Hessian) se width.
WhyWeights pe uncertainty milti hai, aur prediction overconfident nahi hoti. Course plan ka explicit topic, 10-mark derivation possible.
When"Explain Laplace approximation", "Bayesian logistic regression", "predictive distribution", "probit approximation".
How(1) MAP w by Newton–Raphson/IRLS, (2) SN = H−1, H = ΦᵀRΦ + αI, (3) predict with σ(κ(σa²)μa).
The Laplace approximation replaces a density p(z) by the Gaussian centred at its mode z0 with precision A = −∇∇ ln p(z)|z0, i.e. q(z) = N(z | z0, A−1). Kernel functions · kernel trick · support vector machines

S15–S16 · B2 §14.2–14.5
WhatKernel k(x, x′) = φ(x)ᵀφ(x′): high-dimensional features ka dot product, bina φ banaye. SVM = maximum-margin linear classifier jo kernels use karta hai.
WhyLinear method → nonlinear boundary, cheap mein. SVM 10-mark ka classic: margin, support vectors, soft margin, kernel trick.
When"Explain kernel trick with example", "Mercer condition", "hard vs soft margin", "support vectors", "role of C and γ".
HowAlgorithm ko sirf dot products mein likho (dual), phir xᵀz ki jagah k(x, z) daal do. SVM: max 2/‖w‖ s.t. tn(wᵀφn + b) ≥ 1.
A kernel is a function k(x, x′) = φ(x)ᵀφ(x′) that computes an inner product in a feature space; it is valid (Mercer) if every Gram matrix Knm = k(xn, xm) is positive semidefinite. The kernel trick replaces every inner product in an algorithm written in dual form by k(·,·). A support vector machine finds the separating hyperplane with maximum margin 2/‖w‖ by minimising ½‖w‖² subject to tn(wᵀφ(xn) + b) ≥ 1; with slack variables ξn ≥ 0 and penalty C Σξn it becomes the soft-margin SVM. Only the support vectors (points on or inside the margin) have non-zero dual coefficients.
RBF kernel k = exp(−γ‖x − x′‖²): γ bada → har point ka "influence" chhota → wiggly boundary (overfit). C bada → margin violations mehngi → hard margin (overfit). Dono validation se tune karo.
"Kernel trick = data ko higher dimension mein map karna" adhoora answer hai. Point ye hai ki mapping explicitly compute nahi hoti; sirf k(x, z) compute hota hai.
Linear, RBF and polynomial SVMs compared: CV AUC 0.949 / 0.939 / 0.915. RBF grid search picked C = 10, γ = 0.003. Small γ means a nearly linear boundary, consistent with linear models doing just as well on this data.

K-Means clustering

S19 · B1 §9.1
WhatData ko K groups mein baanto: har point apne nearest centroid ka; centroid = apne points ka mean. Repeat till nothing changes.
WhySabse common clustering algorithm, aur EM ka "hard" version. 10-mark numerical trace almost guaranteed.
When"Apply K-means to the data with initial centroids…", "explain the algorithm", "choose K", "limitations".
How(1) init μk, (2) assign each x to argmin‖x − μk‖², (3) μk = mean of its points, (4) repeat until assignments stop changing. J kabhi badhta nahi.
K-Means partitions N points into K clusters by minimising the distortion J = ΣnΣk rnk‖xn − μk‖², where rnk ∈ {0, 1} indicates assignment. It alternates an assignment step (rnk = 1 for the nearest centroid, minimising J w.r.t. r) and an update step (μk = Σn rnkxn / Σn rnk, minimising J w.r.t. μ). Each step cannot increase J, so the algorithm converges, but only to a local minimum that depends on initialisation.
K choose karne ke 3 tareeke: elbow (J vs K ka "kink"), silhouette (max), aur GMM ka BIC (min). Initialisation ke liye k-means++ (door wale points ko seed banao) + multiple restarts.
K-Means spherical, equal-size clusters assume karta hai aur outliers pe mean kheench jaata hai. Features scale karo pehle (StandardScaler), warna attendance (0–100) CGPA (0–10) ko dominate karega.
From-scratch K-Means (k-means++ seeding, Lloyd iterations, 24 iterations to converge) on 15 standardised features, k = 3, scratch vs sklearn ARI 0.86. Personas: High risk (111 students, 74 % at risk), Coasting (129, 9 %), Engaged achievers (160, 11 %). Silhouette 0.166: risk is a continuum, not separate blobs.

Mixtures of Gaussians · the EM algorithm (and its alternative view)

S20–S21 · B1 §9.2–9.4
WhatData = K Gaussians ka weighted mix. Har point ko soft membership (responsibility γ) milti hai, hard 0/1 nahi.
WhyHidden variable wale har model (GMM, HMM, factor analysis) ka training EM se hota hai. Unit 3 + Unit 4 dono mein kaam aata hai.
When"Explain EM with example", "derive E and M steps for GMM", "K-Means vs GMM", "why EM increases likelihood".
HowE-step: γnk = πkN(xn|μk,Σk) / ΣjπjN(xn|μj,Σj). M-step: weighted means, covariances, πk = Nk/N. Repeat.
A Gaussian mixture is p(x) = Σk πk N(x | μk, Σk) with 0 ≤ πk ≤ 1, Σπk = 1. The EM algorithm maximises the likelihood of models with latent variables by alternating the E-step, which computes the posterior of the latent variables (responsibilities γ(znk)) under the current parameters, and the M-step, which re-estimates parameters: Nk = Σnγnk, μk = (1/Nk)Σγnkxn, Σk = (1/Nk)Σγnk(xn − μk)(xn − μk)ᵀ, πk = Nk/N. In the general view, EM maximises Q(θ, θold) = EZ|X,θold[ln p(X, Z | θ)], and each iteration is guaranteed not to decrease ln p(X | θ).

EM sirf local maximum guarantee karta hai, aur ek Gaussian ek point pe collapse ho sakta hai (σ → 0, likelihood → ∞). Isliye multiple restarts + covariance mein chhota regulariser (reg_covar).

WhatPCA: data ko un directions pe project karo jahan variance sabse zyada hai (covariance ke eigenvectors). FA: observed features = few hidden factors + noise.
Why15 features → 2–3 dimensions: visualise, denoise, faster models. "Choose number of components" course plan ka explicit topic hai.
When"Compute principal components of…", "PCA vs factor analysis", "how many components?", "scree plot".
HowCentre → covariance S → eigen-decompose → sort λ ↓ → keep top k → project z = Uᵀ(x − x̄). Explained ratio = λi/Σλ.
Principal component analysis finds orthonormal directions u1, …, uM that maximise the variance of the projected data; they are the eigenvectors of the sample covariance S = (1/(N−1))Σ(xn − x̄)(xn − x̄)ᵀ with the largest eigenvalues λ1 ≥ λ2 ≥ …, and λi is the variance along ui. Factor analysis models x = Wz + μ + ε with z ~ N(0, I) and ε ~ N(0, Ψ), Ψ diagonal, so each observed variable has its own noise; PCA (as probabilistic PCA) corresponds to isotropic noise Ψ = σ²I.

WhatClustering "close" ko kaise naapta hai (distance), clusters achhe hain ya nahi kaise bataate hain (metrics), aur tree-style clustering (dendrogram).

When"Single vs complete linkage", "draw the dendrogram", "silhouette coefficient", "internal vs external validation".
HowAgglomerative: har point alag cluster → closest pair merge → distance matrix update (linkage rule) → repeat. Cut the dendrogram at a height.
Agglomerative hierarchical clustering starts with each point as a cluster and repeatedly merges the two closest clusters, where closeness is defined by the linkage: single d(A,B) = min d(a,b), complete = max d(a,b), average = mean d(a,b), Ward = increase in within-cluster variance. The merge history is drawn as a dendrogram; cutting it at a height gives a flat clustering. Clusterings are evaluated internally (silhouette s(i) = (b(i) − a(i)) / max(a(i), b(i)), Davies–Bouldin, Calinski–Harabasz) or externally against known labels (adjusted Rand index, NMI, purity).

S28–S29 · B1 §13.1–13.2
WhatMarkov chain: aaj ka state sirf kal pe depend karta hai. HMM: state chhupa hua hai (Struggling/Stable/Strong), hum sirf uska output dekhte hain (grade band).
WhyData i.i.d. nahi hai: semester 5 ka SGPA semester 4 se juda hai. Speech, text, student trajectories sab sequential hain.
When"Define HMM and its parameters", "three problems of HMM", "probability of a state sequence", "Markov assumption".
HowHMM λ = (π, A, B). π = start probs, A = state→state, B = state→observation. Rows of A and B sum to 1.
A first-order Markov chain satisfies p(zt | z1, …, zt−1) = p(zt | zt−1), described by an initial distribution π and a transition matrix Ajk = p(zt = k | zt−1 = j). A hidden Markov model adds observations xt generated from the hidden state by emission probabilities Bk(x) = p(xt | zt = k), giving p(X, Z) = p(z1) Πt≥2 p(zt | zt−1) Πt p(xt | zt). The three basic problems are evaluation p(X | λ) (forward algorithm), decoding the most likely Z (Viterbi) and learning λ (Baum–Welch / EM).
State k mein rehne ka expected time = 1/(1 − Akk) (geometric distribution). Akk = 0.8 → average 5 steps.
Markov model mein state dikhta hai; hidden Markov model mein nahi. "HMM mein emission matrix kyun?": kyunki observation state ka noisy signal hai.
Hidden states Struggling / Stable / Strong, observations = SGPA band of each completed semester (Poor < 6.5, Average < 8, Good ≥ 8). Learned P(stay Struggling) = 0.84: expected 1/(1 − 0.84) ≈ 6 semesters in that state if nothing changes. That number is the case for intervening early.

Forward–backward · sum-product · scaling · maximum likelihood (Baum–Welch)

S30–S33 · B1 §13.2.2–13.2.4
WhatForward αt(k) = P(x1..t, zt = k). Backward βt(k) = P(xt+1..T | zt = k). Dono milake γ (har time pe state ki probability) aur ξ (transitions).
WhySaare KT paths enumerate karna impossible; forward–backward O(K²T) mein kar deta hai. Baum–Welch training inhi pe chalta hai.
When"Compute P(O | λ) by the forward algorithm", "explain backward variable", "why scaling?", "Baum–Welch re-estimation formulas".
Howα1 = π·b(x1); αt = (αt−1A)·b(xt). βT = 1; βt = A(b(xt+1)·βt+1). P(O) = ΣαT. γ = αβ/P(O).
The forward–backward algorithm computes α(zt) = p(x1, …, xt, zt) and β(zt) = p(xt+1, …, xT | zt) recursively in O(K²T), giving p(X) = Σk α(zT = k), the state posteriors γ(zt) = α(zt)β(zt)/p(X) and the pairwise posteriors ξ(zt−1, zt) = α(zt−1) A p(xt | zt) β(zt)/p(X). It is the sum-product algorithm applied to the HMM chain. Because α shrinks geometrically, it is normalised at each step by scaling factors ct, with ln p(X) = Σt ln ct. Baum–Welch is EM for HMMs: the E-step computes γ and ξ; the M-step sets πk = γ1(k), Ajk = Σtξt(j,k) / Σtγt(j), Bk(v) = Σt: xt=vγt(k) / Σtγt(k).
Forward trellis for the worked example: O = Poor, Good, Poor. Each node shows αt(k); P(O) = 0.06264 + 0.01948 = 0.08212.
Sum-product (forward–backward) mein Σ hota hai: sab paths ka total. Max-sum (Viterbi) mein Σ ki jagah max: sirf best path. Algorithm same skeleton, ek operator ka fark.
Long sequences pe α underflow karke 0 ho jaata hai (0.1100). Isliye scaling factors ya log-space. Exam mein "why scaling" = "to prevent numerical underflow while keeping ln p(X) = Σ ln ct".

WhatObservations diye hain; sabse likely poora hidden-state path kaunsa hai? Dynamic programming se, sum ki jagah max.

Howδ1 = π·b(x1); δt(k) = maxj[δt−1(j)Ajk]·bk(xt), store argmax as backpointer ψ. End: best final state, then follow ψ backwards.
The Viterbi algorithm finds Z* = argmaxZ p(Z | X) by dynamic programming: δt(k) = maxz1..t−1 p(x1..t, z1..t−1, zt = k) satisfies δt(k) = [maxj δt−1(j) Ajk] bk(xt), with backpointers ψt(k) = argmaxj δt−1(j)Ajk. The path is recovered by backtracking from argmaxk δT(k). It is the max-sum algorithm on the chain, usually run in log space; cost O(K²T).

Linear dynamical systems · the Kalman filter

S34 · B1 §13.3
WhatHMM jaisa hi, lekin hidden state continuous hai aur sab kuch linear + Gaussian. Example: student ka "true ability level" jo dheere badalta hai.
WhyTracking, GPS, smoothing noisy sensors, SGPA forecasting. Kalman filter = forward algorithm for continuous states.
When"Explain LDS / state-space model", "Kalman predict and update equations", "compare LDS with HMM".
HowPredict: m⁻ = Am, P⁻ = APAᵀ + Γ. Update: K = P⁻Cᵀ(CP⁻Cᵀ + Σ)−1, m = m⁻ + K(y − Cm⁻), P = (I − KC)P⁻.
A linear dynamical system has a continuous latent state with zt = A zt−1 + wt, w ~ N(0, Γ), and observations xt = C zt + vt, v ~ N(0, Σ). Because everything is linear-Gaussian, the filtered posterior p(zt | x1..t) stays Gaussian and is computed exactly by the Kalman filter: a predict step (propagate mean and covariance through A, add Γ) and an update step using the Kalman gain Kt = Pt−Cᵀ(CPt−Cᵀ + Σ)−1. The LDS is the continuous-state analogue of the HMM; its parameters are learned with EM and its smoother is the Rauch–Tung–Striebel recursion.

Cheat sheet: last-night revision

Unit 1 · probability

  • Sum rule p(X) = ΣY p(X,Y); product rule p(X,Y) = p(Y|X)p(X)
  • Bayes: P(H|E) = P(E|H)P(H) / ΣhP(E|h)P(h)
  • Independent: P(A,B) = P(A)P(B); cond. indep.: P(A,B|C) = P(A|C)P(B|C)
  • E[X] = Σx p(x); Var = E[X²] − E[X]²; Cov = E[(X−μx)(Y−μy)]; r = Cov/σxσy
  • Quantile q = F−1(q); median = F−1(0.5); z = (x − μ)/σ
  • Curve fit: E = ½Σ(y − t)², RMSE = √(2E/N), regularised + (λ/2)‖w‖²

Unit 2 · linear models

  • wML = (ΦᵀΦ)−1Φᵀt; 1/βML = mean squared residual
  • Ridge: (λI + ΦᵀΦ)−1Φᵀt · MAP of Bayesian LR with λ = α/β
  • BLR: SN−1 = αI + βΦᵀΦ, mN = βSNΦᵀt, σ²(x) = 1/β + φᵀSNφ
  • Discriminant y = wᵀx + w0; distance of boundary from origin −w0/‖w‖
  • Logistic: p(C1|φ) = σ(wᵀφ), σ(a) = 1/(1 + e−a)
  • Laplace: N(wMAP, H−1); probit κ = (1 + πσa²/8)−1/2
  • Kernel: k = φᵀφ′, Gram PSD; RBF exp(−γ‖x − x′‖²); SVM margin 2/‖w‖

Unit 3 · mixtures & clustering

  • K-Means J = ΣΣ rnk‖xn − μk‖²: assign → update → repeat
  • GMM: γnk = πkNk(xn)/ΣjπjNj(xn); Nk = Σγ; μk = Σγx/Nk; πk = Nk/N
  • EM never decreases ln p(X|θ); only local optimum
  • PCA: eigenvectors of S; explained = λi/Σλ; keep k for ≥ 90 %
  • FA: x = Wz + μ + ε, Ψ diagonal (PPCA: σ²I)
  • Linkage: single min · complete max · average mean · Ward Δvariance
  • Silhouette s = (b − a)/max(a, b) ∈ [−1, 1]; BIC = −2 ln L + p ln N (lower better)

Unit 4 · HMM & LDS

  • λ = (π, A, B); params (K−1) + K(K−1) + K(M−1)
  • Forward α1 = π b(x1), αt = (αt−1A) ⊙ b(xt), P(O) = ΣαT
  • Backward βT = 1, βt = A(b(xt+1) ⊙ βt+1)
  • γt = αtβt/P(O); ξt(j,k) = αt(j)Ajkbk(xt+1)βt+1(k)/P(O)
  • Baum–Welch: π = γ1; Ajk = Σξ/Σγj; Bk(v) = Σx=vγ/Σγ
  • Scaling: ln P(O) = Σ ln ct. Viterbi: Σ → max + backpointers
  • Kalman 1-D: P⁻ = P + q; K = P⁻/(P⁻ + r); m = m + K(y − m); P = (1 − K)P⁻
